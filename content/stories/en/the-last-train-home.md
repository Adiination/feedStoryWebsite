By half past ten the floor had emptied out and the building had gone quiet in
the way offices do, all hum and no voices. Meera had stopped pretending to look
at the spreadsheet an hour ago.

"You're going to miss your train," Rohit said, without looking up.

"So are you."

"I live four stops away. You live at the end of the line." He turned his chair
around. "That's not the same risk."

It was the fourth Thursday in a row. Neither of them had said anything about
that, which by now was its own kind of statement — the project could have been
finished on Tuesday and they both knew it, and every week one of them found a
reason for it not to be.

She closed the laptop. Outside, the city was doing its long orange blur, and the
last train home was eleven forty, and it was quarter to eleven.

"Ask me properly," Meera said.

Rohit looked at her for a second longer than was deniable.

"Stay," he said.
