Seven months is not a long time in a life and it is an extremely long time in a
week. Ana had counted it in flights she couldn't afford, in three-hour time
difference, in the specific ache of hearing someone laugh through a phone
speaker and not being in the room for it.

The arrivals hall at Humberto Delgado is badly lit and full of people holding
signs, and she had decided against a sign, and then made one anyway, and then
left it in the car.

He came through the doors at twenty past four with a bag over one shoulder,
scanning, and the thing that undid her was not the moment he saw her. It was the
half-second before — the face of a man who has been travelling for eleven hours
purely to get to one person, and doesn't yet know whether she's there.

"Nine days," Tomás said, when he could.

"Nine days."

"I'm not spending them sightseeing."

"Good," Ana said. "Because I cancelled everything."
